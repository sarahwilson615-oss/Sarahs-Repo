# Application notes for this resume

Use `Sarah_Wilson_Resume.html` (Print → Save as PDF) or the markdown source as your working copy. This version is written for **entry-level insurance verification and adjacent patient-access roles**, not for medical billing/coding jobs that require hospital or payer-portal tenure.

Do not add CMAA, CBCS, or CPC before you start applying. Employers for these door roles care more about customer service, documentation, computer skills, and confidential-information handling than a verification certificate.

Do not add EHR, Epic, ICD-10, Medicare/Medicaid, or insurance-payer portals to the resume. Those terms help ATS only if a recruiter will not treat them as a misrepresentation.

## Apply now when the posting looks like this

These match Walgreens-style and similar door roles:

- High school diploma or bachelor's degree; customer service experience
- Clear written and verbal communication
- Basic computer skills and data entry
- Ability to work with confidential information
- Insurance eligibility/benefits verification **taught on the job** or listed as preferred, not required
- Remote or Texas-based; phone and documentation heavy

Also a reasonable fit:

- Patient Affairs Specialist
- Financial Access Specialist
- Medical Insurance Verification Specialist (if medical billing experience is preferred, not required)

On applications, your closest proof points are:

- Verifying insurance and title documents before a real estate file can close
- Tracking missing items and deadlines
- Phone and email coordination with clients and third parties
- Confidential financial records
- Remote work at Stitch Fix

## Stretch — apply selectively

Prior Authorization Specialist postings that emphasize **willingness to train**, customer service, and documentation can be worth a try. Use the prior-auth headline swap above. In interviews, be direct: you have not processed medical prior auths; you have cleared files against documented coverage and lender/title requirements.

## Skip for now (or treat as later-career targets)

Wait until you have verification tenure before prioritizing postings that **require**:

- 2–4 years of insurance verification or prior authorization in a physician practice, hospital, home health, or specialty pharmacy
- Hands-on payer portals, medical terminology, or EHR (Epic, Cerner, etc.)
- Medicare/Medicaid, claims, or coding as a must-have
- A medical billing/coding certification as a condition of hire

Those include many Senior Insurance Authorization Specialist and Centene-style verification jobs that prefer payer/prior-auth experience. They are a next step after you are already verifying benefits, not the first application wave.

## Cover letter / “additional information” one-liner

You can paste this and tweak the title:

> I do not have hospital billing tenure, but for 6+ years I have managed real estate transaction files: verifying insurance and title documents, tracking outstanding conditions, documenting missing information, and coordinating by phone and email until a file is clear to proceed. I also worked remotely at Stitch Fix and am ready to complete HIPAA and payer-system training.
